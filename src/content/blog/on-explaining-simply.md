Simple explanations have a bad reputation among people who know things, and some of it is earned. Simplify carelessly and you get something that's easy to read and quietly wrong — the reader leaves confident and misinformed, which is worse than confused.

But the reputation is mostly wrong, because most bad simple explanations aren't failing at simplicity. They're failing at **selection**.

## Two ways to shorten an explanation

**Deletion:** cut the parts that are hard. What's left is smooth, short, and load-bearing in the wrong places. The reader can repeat it and can't use it.

**Compression:** find the one mechanism everything else follows from, explain *that* properly, and let the rest go. Nothing important is missing; it's just not stated, because it's derivable.

The difference is whether the reader can now reason about a case you didn't mention. That's the only test I trust.

## The bomber plane test

The best short explanation I know is the WWII bomber story in [[survivorship-bias]]. It's four sentences and it does something remarkable: after hearing it, you can spot the bias in situations that have nothing to do with planes.

That's compression. It didn't skip the hard part — the hard part *is* the story. It found the version of the hard part that fits in a person's head.

Compare "survivorship bias is when your sample is non-representative because failures are excluded." Technically complete. Teaches nothing. You can't run it on a new case, because it's a label, not a mechanism.

## What this asks of you

The reason simple explanations are rare isn't that people are bad writers. It's that compression requires understanding the thing well enough to know what's load-bearing — and you can hide a shaky understanding behind detail far more easily than behind brevity.

Jargon and completeness are excellent camouflage. Four clear sentences are not. That's why writing the short version is a good way to find out whether you actually understood something, and an uncomfortable one.

It's also why every concept on this site starts with one sentence in the summary line. If I can't write that line, the page isn't ready.
