For about two years I had a note in my phone called "words". It had maybe sixty entries. Most were just the word — no meaning, no sentence, no context. I'd write one down while reading, feel briefly clever, and never open the note again.

When I did open it, roughly half the entries were unrecognisable. I had personally written down a word I now could not define. That's a strange feeling, and it's the reason this site exists.

## What was actually broken

The note wasn't the problem. Three things were:

**No meaning attached.** A bare word is a pointer to nothing. By the time I came back, the context that made it clear was gone.

**No sentence.** Definitions don't survive. Sentences do. I remember *liminal* because of an empty airport at 3 a.m., not because of "relating to a threshold."

**No reason to return.** A note you never reopen is a note you never review, and reviewing is the whole mechanism — see [[spaced-repetition]].

## What this is instead

Every word here has to carry its own weight: what it means, at least one sentence I'd actually say, and where it came from when the origin is worth knowing. If I can't write the sentence, I don't understand the word yet, and it doesn't go in.

The concepts section works the same way. I only write one once I can explain it without the source in front of me. That's a much higher bar than "I read about it," and it turns out to be the bar that matters.

## Why it's public

Not because anyone asked. Two reasons, both selfish:

Writing for an imagined stranger makes me clearer than writing for myself. When it's private I can leave a half-thought and know what I meant. In public I have to finish the sentence.

And a page that exists is a page I'll go back to. The private note was invisible, so it stopped being real.

If you found this and it's useful — good. It's open to read, and it isn't open to edit, which keeps it honest as a record of what one person actually learned and when.
