## The plain version

Simple growth adds the same amount each period. Compounding adds a percentage **of the new total**, so each period's gain is bigger than the last.

₹100 growing 10% a year:

| Year | Simple (+₹10/yr) | Compound (×1.1/yr) |
|---|---|---|
| 1  | ₹110 | ₹110 |
| 5  | ₹150 | ₹161 |
| 10 | ₹200 | ₹259 |
| 30 | ₹400 | ₹1,745 |
| 50 | ₹600 | ₹11,739 |

The two lines are nearly identical for years. Then they aren't.

## The part people miss

**Almost all of the payoff sits at the end.** In that table, more than half the final amount arrives in the last ten years of fifty. Which means the enemy of compounding isn't a bad rate — it's stopping early.

## The rule worth memorising

**Rule of 72:** divide 72 by the annual percentage to get the years to double.

- 6% → doubles in 12 years
- 9% → 8 years
- 12% → 6 years

## It isn't only money

The same shape governs anything where today's output becomes tomorrow's input: a language you practise, a reputation, a codebase, a body of notes like this one. Also debt, and also bad habits — compounding is arithmetic, not a moral.
