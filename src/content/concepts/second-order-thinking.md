## First order vs second order

**First-order:** what happens immediately.
**Second-order:** what happens *because of that*, and then because of that.

Most bad decisions are first-order-correct.

| Action | First order | Second order |
|---|---|---|
| Cut prices to win customers | Sales rise | Competitors match; margins gone; you've trained buyers to wait for discounts |
| Add a rule after every mistake | That mistake stops | A rulebook nobody can hold in their head; judgement atrophies |
| Reward developers by lines of code | More code ships | Worse code ships; the person who deletes 2,000 lines is punished |
| Take on debt to grow fast | You grow fast | The debt now decides what you're allowed to do next |

## How to actually run it

Ask **"and then what?"** three times. Most people stop at one.

Then two useful checks:

1. **Who adapts?** Anything involving people will be gamed — not maliciously, just rationally.
2. **What does it cost me later?** Speed borrowed now is usually repaid with interest. See [[compound-interest]] — it runs in both directions.

## The catch

You can't do this for everything; you'd never decide anything. Save it for decisions that are **hard to reverse** or **repeat many times**. For the rest, decide fast and move.
