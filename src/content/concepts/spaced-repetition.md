## The finding underneath it

Hermann Ebbinghaus memorised nonsense syllables on himself in the 1880s and plotted how fast he lost them. The **forgetting curve** drops sharply at first, then flattens.

The useful part: each time you successfully recall something just as it's fading, the curve resets *shallower*. Recall gets cheaper and the memory lasts longer each time.

```
Review 1  →  remember 1 day
Review 2  →  remember 3 days
Review 3  →  remember 1 week
Review 4  →  remember 1 month
Review 5  →  remember 6 months
```

## Why it works

Two things, and only one is the spacing:

1. **Retrieval, not review.** Pulling an answer out of your head strengthens it. Re-reading a page feels like learning and mostly isn't — it builds familiarity, which is easy to mistake for knowledge.
2. **Desirable difficulty.** The review must be slightly hard. If recall is effortless you waited too little; if it's impossible you waited too long.

## How this applies to a page of words

Collecting a word does almost nothing on its own. What fixes it is **using it** — which is why every entry here carries example sentences rather than a definition alone. Reading `petrichor` in a sentence you wrote is a retrieval; reading its definition again is not.

The honest version of the schedule for a site like this: reread yesterday's words tomorrow, last week's on the weekend, and let the [[apophenia]]-adjacent pleasure of noticing them in the wild do the rest.
