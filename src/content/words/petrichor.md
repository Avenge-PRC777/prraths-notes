---
word: petrichor
pronunciation: /ˈpɛtrɪkɔːr/
partOfSpeech: noun
meaning: The earthy smell that rises when rain falls on dry ground.
examples:
  - "She opened the window at the first sound of thunder, waiting for the petrichor."
  - "Nothing in the city smells like the petrichor of a village after a long summer."
etymology: Coined in 1964 by two Australian scientists from Greek *petra* (stone) and *ichor* — the fluid said to run in the veins of the gods. Literally, the blood of stones.
tags: [noun, sensory, weather, favourite]
added: 2026-08-14
---

The word was invented, not inherited — which is rare and slightly wonderful. The smell itself comes from oils that plants release in dry spells, which soak into the soil and get kicked back into the air by raindrops.
